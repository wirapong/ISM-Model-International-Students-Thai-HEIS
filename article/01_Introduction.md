## 1. Introduction

### Paragraph 1 — Opening: Problem and Significance

International student mobility has expanded dramatically over the past two decades, driven by the forces of globalization and a growing demand for internationally recognized qualifications (Knight, 2008; Qiang, 2003). In Thailand, this trend is particularly pronounced: the Office of Higher Education Commission (OHEC) reported approximately 19,000 international students enrolled across more than 155 institutions, nearly three-quarters of whom are Chinese nationals (Kang et al., 2019; OHEC, 2015). Thailand has accordingly pursued a strategic ambition to establish itself as a regional higher education hub within the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN), expanding international program offerings and promoting cross-border educational cooperation (Chang, 2011; Jampaklay et al., 2022). Despite this growth, international students in Thailand frequently encounter multifaceted challenges — including language barriers, cultural adjustment difficulties, limited access to institutional information, and situational obstacles — that impede their academic performance and overall well-being (Berry, 1997; Kang et al., 2019; Rujiprak & Limprasert, 2016). Effective Information Services Management (ISM) has emerged as a critical institutional response to these challenges, yet empirical understanding of how institutional quality factors systematically shape international students' adaptation and information experiences in the Thai higher education context remains limited.

### Paragraph 2 — Prior Research: What We Know

Prior research on international student experiences in higher education has advanced along three interrelated dimensions. First, studies on student satisfaction have consistently documented that academic and educational quality, administrative and staff support, and institutional image are pivotal drivers of international students' satisfaction and overall institutional evaluation (Arambewela & Hall, 2009; Hammons et al., 2004; Ngwira & Intravisit, 2023; Qadeer et al., 2021; Wong & Chapman, 2023). Second, research on cross-cultural adjustment has established that the stress of culture shock (Berry, 1997), psychological adaptation processes (Bethel et al., 2020; Zheng & Ishii, 2023), and sociocultural integration (Rujiprak & Limprasert, 2016; Sangkhao et al., 2019) collectively determine international students' long-term adaptation outcomes. A third stream of scholarship has focused on student information needs and seeking behavior, finding that access to relevant institutional information, information-seeking strategies, and situational barriers significantly affect students' academic engagement and self-development (Bukhari et al., 2018; Hertzum & Hyldegård, 2019; Yoon & Chung, 2017). Collectively, these bodies of evidence confirm that international students' experiences are shaped by a confluence of institutional, cultural, and informational factors (Martirosyan, 2019; Veerasamy & Ammigan, 2022).

### Paragraph 3 — Research Gap: What Remains Unknown

Notwithstanding these contributions, three critical gaps persist. First, while prior studies have examined institutional quality and student adaptation in isolation, the **causal pathways** through which institutional input factors simultaneously influence multiple adaptation and information outcome domains have not been empirically established within a single integrated model. Research to date tends to treat student satisfaction, cultural adjustment, and information needs as separate phenomena, precluding a holistic understanding of how they interact within an ISM framework (Madden-Dent et al., 2019; Moscaritolo et al., 2022). Second, the majority of existing studies have been conducted in English-speaking Western contexts — particularly the United States, United Kingdom, and Australia (Ammigan & Jones, 2018) — where the institutional culture, linguistic environment, and administrative structures differ substantially from those of Thailand. Direct applicability of these findings to the Thai context is therefore limited. Third, no empirically validated ISM model currently exists that links institutional quality factors to the full spectrum of international students' psychological, sociocultural, informational, and situational experiences in Thai higher education. This gap is consequential: without understanding these causal mechanisms, universities and policymakers cannot effectively prioritize institutional investments or design targeted support interventions (Jampaklay et al., 2022; Nguyen & Tuamsuk, 2022).

### Paragraph 4 — Theoretical Framework: Rationale for the Model

This study draws on three complementary theoretical frameworks to construct and test a Direct/Parallel ISM model. The **Service Quality framework (SERVQUAL)**, adapted to the educational context, informs the operationalization of two institutional antecedent constructs: Academic and Education Quality (AR), encompassing the academic resources, curriculum quality, and instructional standards that the institution provides; and Administrative and Staff Support (HS), reflecting the responsiveness, accessibility, and competence of administrative services for international students (Parasuraman et al., 1988; Qadeer et al., 2021). **Berry's (1997) cross-cultural adaptation theory** provides the theoretical basis for the two adaptation outcome constructs: Psychological Adaptation (PA), representing students' emotional adjustment and mental well-being; and Sociocultural Adaptation (LC), reflecting competence in navigating the host culture's social norms and practices. **Information science theories** (Borko, 1968; Saracevic, 2009) ground the remaining two outcome constructs: Information Needs (IA), capturing the degree to which the institution satisfies students' information-seeking requirements; and Situational Issue (SI), encompassing the contextual challenges and practical barriers students encounter in their daily academic and social lives. Theoretically, higher institutional quality in academic and administrative domains is expected to reduce psychological stress, facilitate cultural integration, fulfill information requirements, and mitigate situational obstacles — establishing the rationale for direct structural paths from AR and HS to each of the four endogenous constructs simultaneously.

### Paragraph 5 — Research Objectives and Hypotheses

This study pursues two primary objectives: (1) to examine the direct effects of Academic and Education Quality (AR) and Administrative and Staff Support (HS) on international students' Psychological Adaptation (PA), Sociocultural Adaptation (LC), Information Needs satisfaction (IA), and Situational Issue management (SI) in Thai higher education institutions; and (2) to construct and empirically validate an Information Services Management (ISM) model that integrates these relationships within a coherent structural framework. The following eight hypotheses guide the analysis:

**Hypotheses related to Academic and Education Quality (AR):**

- **H1a:** Academic and Education Quality has a positive direct influence on Psychological Adaptation.
- **H1b:** Academic and Education Quality has a positive direct influence on Sociocultural Adaptation.
- **H1c:** Academic and Education Quality has a positive direct influence on Information Needs satisfaction.
- **H1d:** Academic and Education Quality has a positive direct influence on Situational Issue management.

**Hypotheses related to Administrative and Staff Support (HS):**

- **H3a:** Administrative and Staff Support has a positive direct influence on Psychological Adaptation.
- **H3b:** Administrative and Staff Support has a positive direct influence on Sociocultural Adaptation.
- **H3c:** Administrative and Staff Support has a positive direct influence on Information Needs satisfaction.
- **H3d:** Administrative and Staff Support has a positive direct influence on Situational Issue management.

### Paragraph 6 — Contributions and PLS-SEM Justification

This study offers contributions across three domains. **Theoretically**, it extends ISM scholarship by integrating three distinct theoretical dimensions — Student Satisfaction, Cultural Adjustment, and Student Needs — into a unified causal model, thereby demonstrating how institutional quality factors simultaneously drive multiple student outcome domains within the Thai higher education context. This integration advances theoretical understanding beyond single-dimension accounts by revealing the breadth and magnitude of institutional influence on international student experiences. **Empirically**, the study provides evidence from 354 international students across four types of Thai universities — autonomous, private, Rajabhat, and technology and occupational institutions — using a validated 22-item instrument and rigorous bootstrapped PLS-SEM inference, thereby addressing both the geographical and contextual gaps in the existing literature. **Practically**, the findings provide actionable guidance for Thai university administrators and the Office of Higher Education Commission (OHEC) on how to allocate institutional resources most effectively: by identifying which institutional quality dimensions exert the strongest direct effects on specific student outcome domains, the ISM model enables institutions to design targeted interventions rather than generic support programs.

PLS-SEM is employed as the analytical technique because the study simultaneously pursues the prediction and explanation of multiple endogenous constructs (PA, LC, IA, and SI) within a Direct/Parallel structural model — a research design for which PLS-SEM's variance-based approach and PLSc algorithm are particularly well-suited (Hair et al., 2019; Ringle et al., 2015). The exploratory nature of ISM model development in the Thai context, combined with the study's focus on identifying the relative predictive contribution of each institutional factor across diverse outcome domains, further supports the selection of PLS-SEM over covariance-based SEM approaches (Hair et al., 2019).
